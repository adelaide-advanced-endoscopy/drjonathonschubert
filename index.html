About Dr Schubert

Expertise built around advanced endoscopy

Dr Jonathon Schubert is a Consultant Gastroenterologist and Interventional Endoscopist with a particular focus on advanced diagnostic and therapeutic endoscopy.

Following specialist training in South Australia, Dr Schubert completed an Interventional Endoscopy Fellowship at the Royal Brisbane and Women's Hospital, followed by advanced ESD training in leading centres in Japan and Germany.

He leads the advanced endoscopy service at Southern Adelaide Local Health Network (SALHN) and established the SALHN Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection program following his return to Adelaide in 2024. His practice includes the assessment and endoscopic treatment of complex mucosal lesions and selected early gastrointestinal cancers.

Dr Schubert has a background in Engineering (First Class Honours) and a PhD from the University of Adelaide focused on Helicobacter pylori and gastric cancer risk. He brings an analytical, evidence-based approach to clinical decision-making while placing considerable emphasis on communication, safety and the experience of each patient.

He works collaboratively with General Practitioners, gastroenterologists, gastrointestinal surgeons and other specialists to provide appropriate investigation, treatment and follow-up.

Patient-centred care

Expertise is only part of good care

Advanced procedures can be complex. The aim is not simply to perform a technically difficult procedure, but to make sure each patient understands why it is being recommended, what to expect and how they will be supported before and after treatment.

01

Time to understand

Patients should understand their diagnosis, the purpose of a procedure, the expected benefits and the relevant risks before proceeding.

02

Individualised decisions

Not every lesion or condition requires an advanced procedure. Treatment is selected according to the patient's circumstances, anatomy, pathology and preferences.

03

Minimally invasive where appropriate

For appropriately selected lesions, advanced endoscopic techniques may offer an organ-preserving alternative to surgery.

04

Follow-up matters

Care continues after the procedure, with pathology review, surveillance planning and communication with the patient's usual doctors.

Patient information

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a referral?

A referral is generally required for specialist medical consultation and is useful in ensuring that relevant clinical information and previous investigations are available before your appointment.

Can I be referred specifically for ESD or EMR?

Yes. Patients with large or complex gastrointestinal lesions can be referred for an advanced endoscopic opinion. The most appropriate treatment is determined after review of the clinical history, endoscopic appearance and pathology where available.

Is an advanced procedure always better than surgery?

No. Advanced endoscopic treatment is appropriate only for selected patients and lesions. Treatment recommendations take into account the depth and nature of a lesion, technical considerations, cancer risk, patient preferences and the alternatives available.

What happens after an ESD or EMR?

Pathology is reviewed following resection and an appropriate surveillance or further-treatment plan is developed. Results and recommendations are communicated to the patient and referring doctor.

Where are procedures performed?

Procedures are performed at selected accredited private hospital and day-hospital locations in Adelaide. The appropriate location depends on the procedure and the patient's individual requirements.
